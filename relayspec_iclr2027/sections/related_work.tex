\paragraph{Target-conditioned drafting.}
Medusa predicts future positions with additional heads on the target's final hidden states~\citep{cai2024medusa}. EAGLE uses target features for autoregressive drafting, with later versions improving tree construction and training~\citep{li2024eagle,li2024eagle2,li2025eagle3}. ReDrafter combines target-conditioned recurrent drafting with beam search and dynamic tree attention~\citep{cheng2024redrafter}. DFlash predicts a masked block in parallel and supplies target context to every draft layer~\citep{chen2026dflash}. RelaySpec starts from a pretrained DFlash checkpoint and studies its reuse after the target changes.

\paragraph{Adapting to changed targets.}
DistillSpec studies training data and divergence objectives for matching a drafter to its target~\citep{zhou2024distillspec}. Online Speculative Decoding updates the drafter from serving feedback~\citep{liu2024online}. Efficient Draft Adaptation separates shared and target-specific components and trains the private component using regenerated and selected data~\citep{lin2026eda}. OmniDraft addresses vocabulary mismatch with an online cache and hybrid distillation~\citep{ramakrishnan2025omnidraft}. These methods already address parts of the reuse problem. RelaySpec asks how much adaptation can be placed in five maps before a pretrained DFlash fusion projection while retaining the draft transformer.

\paragraph{Parallel drafting across targets.}
PARD trains an autoregressive model for parallel prediction and supports reuse within a target family~\citep{an2026pard}. PARD-2 supports both target-conditioned and target-independent drafting~\citep{an2026pard2}. RelaySpec instead starts with a target-conditioned DFlash checkpoint and adapts it to changed targets. Our experiments compare separate and pooled adaptation because target-specific training is not automatically necessary or better.

\paragraph{Steering pretrained drafters.}
SD$^2$ uses linear projections of target hidden states to add steering signals inside a pretrained autoregressive drafter's feed-forward layers~\citep{berdoz2026steering}. Its main method trains the steering projections and draft weights jointly. Its frozen-drafter ablation also improves acceptance, although joint training performs better in that comparison. This establishes prior evidence for steering both trainable and frozen drafters. RelaySpec studies DFlash input maps that fold into the fusion projection, comparing token supervision for specialization with reconstruction for cross-model transfer.

\paragraph{Linear maps and training-time parameterization.}
\citet{huang2025cross} use linear transformations to transfer concept-steering vectors across language models. \citet{chen2025stitching} learn affine maps between model activations to transfer features, predictors, and steering vectors. These results motivate mapping hidden states, but do not establish that a mapped drafter retains its acceptance or speed. RepSpec adds redundant linear structures during draft training and merges them for inference~\citep{huo2026repspec}. RelaySpec shares the use of mergeable linear operations, but trains maps on target-feature inputs while preserving the pretrained draft transformer. Our direct-fusion comparisons and matched-weight gradient diagnostics examine how this parameterization changes optimization. They do not attribute a trained checkpoint's advantage to a single gradient measurement.
